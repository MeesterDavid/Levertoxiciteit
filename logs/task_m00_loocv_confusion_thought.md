## 2. Methodological & scientific considerations
- **Why LOOCV?** With n = 12 and 3 classes, any in-sample accuracy is meaningless; leave-one-out is the
  standard unbiased estimator for tiny panels and is exactly what a probabilistic risk assessment wants:
  it exposes the residual uncertainty (mean P(true class) = 0.39 instead of 0.98).
- **What the matrix shows (computed from the cache):** Graphormer LOOCV = 8/12, mean P 0.39 (0.18-0.51);
  ToxTransformer LOOCV = 8/12, mean P 0.55 (0.01-0.94); dual consensus LOOCV = 8/12, mean P 0.47
  (0.10-0.72). The *class* decisions are identical in all three (same matrix), only the probabilities
  differ: rows 3/0/1, 0/2/2, 0/1/3. Total errors = 4 (Fenofibrate -> MIE 41; Amiodarone and Tamoxifen
  -> MIE 41 instead of 167; Chlorpromazine -> MIE 167 instead of 41). This is a biologically meaningful
  answer: the two MIE classes share a lipophilic/amphiphilic pharmacophore, so a confusion is expected and
  exactly why the dose-dependent Neural ODE (M3/M4) is needed.
- **In-sample is removed, not hidden:** the calibrated head probabilities remain in the JSON/`.pt` as the
  model output that feeds Milestone 2; they are simply no longer advertised as validation evidence.

## 6. Reflection & Next Step
- The reported score is now the leave-one-out one everywhere (console, JSON, dashboard, slide, speaker
  notes) and it tells a *useful* story: 8/12 with mean P(true class) 0.39, and a confusion matrix that
  shows the LXR/BSEP classes are chemically entangled. That is a much stronger argument for the
  dose-dependent Neural ODE than a perfect (and meaningless) in-sample diagonal.
- Worth noting for the defence: LOOCV here validates the *nearest-prototype* classifier in the frozen
  embedding space, not the calibrated heads - i.e. it measures how separable the MIE classes are in the
  pretrained representation. That is the honest quantity for n = 12 and should be phrased as such.
